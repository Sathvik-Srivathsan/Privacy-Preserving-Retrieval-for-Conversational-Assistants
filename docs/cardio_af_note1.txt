Mrs. K. presented to the cardiology clinic with a three-month history of palpitations that come on without warning, mostly in the evening. She described the sensation as her heart fluttering and skipping beats, and she has noticed increasing fatigue by mid-afternoon that she attributes to poor sleep. On brief exertion, such as climbing the stairs to the second floor, she becomes noticeably short of breath. Her blood pressure has been well controlled on amlodipine, and there is no history of chest pain. An ambulatory ECG recording captured intermittent atrial fibrillation with a ventricular rate ranging from 74 to 138 beats per minute, and the rhythm did not self-convert during the nine-day monitoring window.

We reviewed rate control with metoprolol and discussed rhythm-control options given her symptomatic burden. Because her CHA2DS2-VASc score is elevated, anticoagulation with apixaban was initiated rather than aspirin. She has been advised to report any new chest discomfort or a fast, irregular pulse that does not settle.
