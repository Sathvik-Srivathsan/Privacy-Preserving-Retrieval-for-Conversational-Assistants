Mr. D., aged 58, presented to the emergency assessment unit with central chest pressure that began while he was sweeping his garage. The pain was dull, non-pleuritic, and radiated to the left shoulder; it did not change with deep inspiration but worsened when he walked to the triage desk. His blood pressure on arrival was 146 over 92 in the right arm, with no significant difference between limbs. The initial ECG showed subtle T-wave flattening in the lateral leads, and the first troponin was at the upper limit of normal. Serial troponin measurements taken two and four hours after arrival remain within the high-sensitivity reference range, so we are leaning toward stable angina rather than infarction.

A stress test is scheduled for next week. In the meantime, aspirin 81 mg daily and sublingual nitroglycerin for chest pain were prescribed, with instructions to present again immediately if the pain becomes constant or is accompanied by sweating, nausea, or new breathlessness.
